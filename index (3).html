Our Mission

Our Mission

Roots2Harvest Foundation exists to reconnect communities to the land through agricultural education and community-based participatory research, building food sovereignty and health sovereignty from the ground up.

We serve Birmingham, Alabama — a city where 70% of neighborhoods are classified as food deserts — and are building toward regional impact across the Southeast. Our work sits at the intersection of ecology, public health, and economic justice. We believe communities deserve not only access to fresh food, but the knowledge, infrastructure, and research to sustain it for generations.

Our Programs

Roots2Harvest Foundation is our community and research engine — holding educational initiatives, building pathways for community entrepreneurship, and leading community-based participatory research.

Community Education Series

A monthly series demonstrating growing practices and the challenges of urban agriculture in the South. Each session is led by experienced growers — master gardeners and farmers from the region — providing hands-on learning rooted in local knowledge and conditions.

NRG — Inner-G Program

"What you put your energy into GROWS." NRG employs and trains individuals stigmatized by previous convictions in urban agriculture. Each cohort partners together to establish and operate an agricultural or value-added business — with every member as a co-owner. Workforce development rooted in dignity and shared wealth.

Community-Based Participatory Research

We lead research measuring how fresh food intervention impacts our communities — at the level of environmental resilience, community connectedness and social health, and individual mental and physiological health. Our current focus is the restoration of urban soils for agricultural safety in Birmingham.

Our Story

Roots2Harvest Foundation was born from a scientist's urgency and a community's need.

  • The Science

    From Cells to Ecosystems

    Dr. Amanda D. Clark spent years investigating life at the cellular level as a molecular biologist and at the species level as an evolutionary biologist. Across both scales, the same pattern emerged: our environment — our nutrition, where we live, our habits — profoundly shapes our health and happiness.

  • The Problem

    Birmingham's Food Reality

    What alarmed Dr. Clark most was the overlap of food insecurity and chronic disease in Birmingham — a city where 70% of neighborhoods are classified as food deserts. The data was undeniable. The science pointed directly to the need for food and health infrastructure built for long-term community resilience.

  • The Turn

    From Academia to Action

    After years in academic research, Dr. Clark decided to make a more direct impact — by growing food, providing educational resources, and using her scientific breadth to lead research with her communities, not just about them. Community-based participatory research became the foundation of everything.

  • 2026

    Roots2Harvest Foundation Is Born

    Roots2Harvest Foundation was formally established in Birmingham, Alabama, with a commitment to food sovereignty, health sovereignty, and community-led research — serving Birmingham today and building toward regional impact across the Southeast.

Dr. Amanda D. Clark — Founder & Executive Director

Dr. Clark is a molecular and evolutionary biologist whose research across scales of life — from cells to ecosystems — revealed the deep connection between environment, nutrition, and health. She founded Roots2Harvest Foundation to translate that scientific understanding into direct community impact through urban agriculture, education, and participatory research in Birmingham.

Quinton Harvill — Innovation & Operations Development Officer

Quinton Harvill is an innovator focused on sustainable real estate development, education, and community-centered business models. He brings expertise in engineering modeling and construction to Roots2Harvest, leading the design and development of operational systems, physical infrastructure, and scalable frameworks that allow the organization to grow sustainably.

Grace Wettstone — Community Engagement & Wellness Officer

Grace holds a degree in social work with a focus in social psychology and brings experience in education, early childhood development, and recreation therapy for adults in mental health settings. She leads wellness programming at our community garden sites, guides the relational care component of our senior food programs, and ensures the organization responds to the full range of what the communities we serve are carrying.
